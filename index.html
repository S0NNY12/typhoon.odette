What to do Before, During, and After a Typhoon

This project focuses on educating communities about typhoons and how to stay safe during such disasters.

The primary beneficiaries are residents of typhoon-prone areas who need better preparation strategies.

The program is funded through partnerships with local government units and international organizations like UN Habitat.

Introduction to Typhoon Rai/Odette


Locally known as Typhoon Odette in the Philippines, it originated as a tropical disturbance near the equator dating at December 10, 2021. It intensified as it moved to the west, entering the PAR (Philippine Area of Responsibility) on December 14th, before making landfall firstly in Siargao on December 16th as a category 5 typhoon. Maximum sustained winds reached 195 km/h, with gustiness going up to 270 km/h, becoming one of the strongest storms to hit the Phillippines in 2021, as going for third strongest recorded in the Nothern Hemisphere.


Typhoon Odette left widespread destruction, devastation of power supply, housing, and agriculture, affecting millions of Filipinos. When December 18th came, the center was located near Kalayaan Island Group and had traveled distances from areas like Palawan, Puerto Princesa City, Nha Trang, and even Vietnam. The Odette storm's rapid intensification and destruction impact highlights its significant place in Meteorological records.

Solutions to Mitigate Typhoon Impacts


Img provided by: IFRC GO., (2022, January 25). Philippines - Typhoon Rai (Odette).

Typhoon Rai (Odette) caused severe destruction, killing over 400 people and damaging homes, infrastructure, and farmland. Bohol suffered significant damage from Typhoon Odette. Many homes were destroyed, especially in rural areas. Flooding and landslides damaged roads and farmland. Power and communication lines were down for weeks, making recovery difficult. The total cost of damage to agriculture and infrastructure attributed to Typhoon Odette has climbed to more than PHP 28 billion, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) said Monday. Approximately 2,108,858 houses, of which 404,653 were destroyed and 1,704,205 were partially damaged. The damage to crops, livestock, poultry, fisheries, and agricultural facilities was estimated to be worth PHP 17.8 billion, while the infrastructure damage was estimated to be worth PHP 30 billion.

Chosen Solution


Proposed Solution to Mitigate the Impact of Typhoons

POSSIBLE SOLUTIONS:
Better help from communities
Better environment
More available resources to endure floods
Being mindful of the effects of typhoons

EVALUATION: Based on what we chose for the list of solutions, our group decided that the best solution is for communities to be better involved before, during, and after a typhoon in order to mitigate the impacts of typhoons. Involvement is crucial at times like these, according to OCHA., (2021, December 24)., as evidenced by the aftermath of Typhoon Odette (Rai) in December 2021. Approximately 16 million across six regions in the Phillippines were affected, leaving 2.4 million Filipinos in need of urgent care.

VALIDATING THE SOLUTION: The challenges faced by Typhoon Odette necessitate an active and coordinative disaster response. As the Humanitaries Needs and Priorities document called for 169M dollars to address urgent needs over six months, highlighting the required resources in the absence of sufficient pre-disaster preparedness.

Prototype


Our initiative, SOS: Students Organizing Supplies, focuses on gathering donations of essential items for families in need, such as food, aid kits, and clothing. For those who can't contribute directly, we invite them to help source donations. We'll expand our outreach through engaging videos and set up donation boxes and bags for students. Donation locations will be available near school grounds for contributions at any time.
